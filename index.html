01 / The big idea

Trading means buying and selling.

In financial markets, people trade assets such as shares or currencies, hoping to benefit from changes in their prices. A profit is possible, but so is a loss.

01

Buy an asset

Imagine buying one company share for £100. A share represents a small ownership stake in that company.

02

The price changes

News, company results, interest rates and expectations can affect what buyers will pay and sellers will accept.

03

Sell and see the result

Sell at £110 and you make £10. Sell at £90 and you lose £10. These examples exclude fees and taxes.

Trading vs investing

Trading usually focuses on shorter-term price changes. Investing generally involves holding assets for longer, often years. Both involve risk; neither guarantees a return.

02 / What can people trade?

Different markets. Different assets.

STOCKS

A piece of a company

Shares are ownership stakes in businesses. Their prices can respond to earnings, growth expectations and wider economic conditions.

Example: a company share

FOREX

One currency for another

Currencies trade in pairs. GBP/USD expresses the value of the British pound in US dollars. A rising pair means the pound is strengthening relative to the dollar.

Example: GBP/USD

COMMODITIES

Physical goods

Markets also trade goods such as gold, oil and wheat, often through financial contracts rather than physical delivery. Supply and demand influence prices.

Example: gold or crude oil

04 / Understand the downside

Risk is part of every trade.

You can lose money

A price can move against you. A share can lose all its value, so a successful trade should never be treated as certain.

Borrowing increases exposure

Leverage lets someone control a larger position with less initial money. It magnifies gains and losses and can use up the initial funds quickly.

Costs affect the result

Broker charges and the spread—the difference between buying and selling prices—can reduce returns. Tax treatment also depends on the circumstances.

What is a stop-loss?

A stop-loss is an instruction intended to close a position if the price moves against you to a chosen level. The final execution price can differ during gaps or fast markets, so it does not guarantee a maximum loss.

Does a rising chart guarantee another rise?

No. A chart shows past prices. It can help someone study price behaviour, but it cannot guarantee what happens next.
